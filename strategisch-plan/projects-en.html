Strategic projects

#1
Faculty portfolio and future-proof curricula
+

We are developing a portfolio of programmes that gives graduates deep domain expertise and the skills to tackle tomorrow's challenges. For our bachelor and master programmes this means future-proof curricula that shape 'key-shaped' professionals: a broad foundation combined with one or more specialisations, ready for lifelong learning. We pay particular attention to transferable skills, the responsible and effective use of AI, interdisciplinarity and internationalisation. For our advanced and complementary programmes we safeguard their positioning and relevance to professional practice. All of this without increasing the workload for staff.

#2
Stepping stones for internationalisation
+

We embed internationalisation in all programmes following the stepping stone principle: a coherent, progressive build-up of international experiences throughout the study trajectory, growing in scope and complexity. It ranges from internationalisation at home (international cases, international guest lecturers) to virtual mobility and virtual exchange, study trips, Blended Intensive Programmes (BIPs) and summer schools. The aim is more participation in semester mobility, international internships and double-degree master's programmes.

#9
Towards an inspiring, sustainable and inclusive campus
+

This project is part of Ghent University's campus vision for 2050: bringing the eleven faculties together on three core campuses and renovating the university's buildings. The Faculty of Economics and Business Administration will move, together with the Faculty of Engineering and Architecture and the Faculty of Bioscience Engineering, to new buildings on the Ardoyen Campus. FEB's move is currently scheduled for 2038. The three faculties are jointly developing an 'Ardoyen 2040' vision, which together with a campus plan will lead to a spatial programme of requirements. Meanwhile we gather our own insights into how spaces are used today, to help design inspiring, sustainable and inclusive buildings later on.

Contributes to:1.2 An inclusive and supportive learning environment1.3 Stimulate educational innovation2.2 Infusing practice with knowledge3.1 Stronger connections, greater impact4.1 Engaged and proud employees

Most recently completed action

March 2026

De Huiskamer: a social space for staff

The move to the Ardoyen Campus is still about ten years away. Until then we want our current buildings to remain a pleasant place to work. A social space for staff had been on the wish list since 2017, but there was no suitable room. When the caretaker's apartment became vacant, the opportunity arose. The fourth floor of the Hoveniersberg building was turned into a lounge with a kitchen, seating areas, around twenty seats and workstations by the windows, a meeting room for on-site and online meetings, and a green terrace overlooking the city. Works started in October 2025 and De Huiskamer opened in March 2026. Ghent University (Campus and Buildings) handled the renovation, the faculty funded the furniture and equipment. The space is used intensively throughout the day, and the terrace is very popular in summer.
